# Paper notes: the baseline Lights builds on

Source: Clemente, M. C., Dela Cruz, J., Mendoza, K. R., **Nudo, K. M.**, Ancheta, C. Jr., Garrido, L., Bonifacio, J., Bustarde, J., Castro, E., Macam, A. (Saint Louis University, Baguio City). *Temporal Analysis of Nighttime Lights and Vegetation Indices for Poverty Forecasting: A Recurrent Neural Network Perspective.* 10 pages (Springer LNNS chapter, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-23515-2_43; page numbers refer to the author's PDF, read 2026-10-21).

These notes record the paper's method as written, because the Lights baseline reproduces it before extending it.

## 1. One-paragraph summary

The paper trains a single-layer recurrent neural network to forecast the **Population in the Lowest Wealth Quintile (PLWQ)** of each Philippine **administrative region** (first-level units, FAO GAUL boundaries) from three monthly time series: **pixel-level radiance (PLRV)** from DMSP-OLS nighttime lights, and **NDVI** and **EVI** from MODIS MOD13Q1 (p. 3–5). The target comes from the **Demographic and Health Survey (DHS)** wealth index, available only for survey years (1993–2017), so monthly values of both the lights and the target are **imputed with Prophet** (p. 5). The model is evaluated with a random 80/20 split and MAE/MSE/MAPE averaged over regions (p. 5, 7–8). Reported PLWQ errors are RMSE 0.1175, MAE 0.4878, MAPE 2.7544 (p. 8). The authors state it is "the first model to conduct a time-series analysis and construct a predictive, high-accuracy RNN model for poverty forecasting, particularly in the Philippines" (p. 8) and explicitly list **municipal-level analysis** as future work (p. 9).

## 2. Research questions (p. 2)

1. Accuracy of the model in forecasting PLWQ.
2. How PLRV, EVI, NDVI and PLWQ vary over time, and their temporal patterns.
3. The relationship between PLRV, EVI and NDVI.

## 3. Data (p. 3–4, Table 2)

| Variable | Source | Years | Resolution / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target: **PLWQ**, "proportion of individuals in a region classified as belonging to the lowest wealth category" (p. 2) | Philippine DHS wealth index (asset-based quintiles) | 1993, 1998, 2003, 2008, 2013, 2017 | Regional. "As of present, the latest available data was from 2017" (p. 3) |
| Boundaries | FAO GAUL first-level administrative units, via Earth Engine | 2015 | Regions, not provinces or municipalities (p. 3–4) |
| **NDVI, EVI** | MOD13Q1.061 Terra Vegetation Indices 16-day, via Earth Engine | 2001–2022 | 250 m, 16-day (p. 3–4) |
| **PLRV** (nighttime lights) | **DMSP-OLS Nighttime Lights Time Series, Version 4** | 2004–2013 | 927.67 m (p. 3) |

Preprocessing (p. 4–5):
- MODIS and lights are reduced to **monthly means per region** ("Monthly mean PLRV values were extracted for each administrative region", p. 4; "requires conversion to monthly means", p. 5).
- "The DHS survey was conducted every 3-5 years, while the nighttime light data was available in monthly increments for 2004-2012. In order to replace missing monthly PLRV and PLWQ values, statistical imputation was implemented using predictions generated by the Prophet model." (p. 5). Prophet fills both the predictor gaps (lights after 2013) and the target gaps (non-survey months) before training.
- Predictors were "already on the same scale, requiring no further transformations", while PLWQ was normalised (p. 5).

## 4. Model (p. 5, Fig. 1)

- "A Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) model was employed to forecast PLWQ, with PLRV, NDVI, and EVI as input predictor variables."
- Cell type: "The RNN model utilized Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) cells to capture temporal patterns. The model consisted of **1 GRU layer** and underwent training for **100 epochs**." (p. 5). Table 1 (p. 3) also lists a "Marginally Stable Triangular RNN" (Sivakumar & Sivakumar 2018) among related models, but the method section does not say it was used. **The baseline reproduction uses one GRU layer** and records an LSTM variant as a sensitivity run.
- Optimiser: **Adam, learning rate 0.001, batch size 32** (p. 5). Hidden size, sequence length, loss function and framework are chosen in the reproduction (see MODELING-PLAN.md).
- Target normalised; inputs not transformed (p. 5).

## 5. Validation and metrics (p. 5)

- "The model underwent validation through cross-validation, where **80% of the data was allocated for training purposes, and the remaining 20% was used for testing**." The reproduction reads this as a random split over region-months. Lights adds leave-years-out and spatial-block validation on top of it (see MODELING-PLAN.md).
- Metrics: MAE, MSE, MAPE (p. 5); the results table reports RMSE, MAE and MAPE (p. 7–8).
- Errors are computed for the predictors as well as the target ("the error of pixel-radiance values, EVI, NDVI, and the target variable, PLWQ, was measured by comparing the forecasted values against the actual values in the test set", p. 5), i.e. the network forecasts all four series.
- A correlation analysis between the predictors was also done (p. 5, Fig. 6).

## 6. Results (p. 6–8)

Table 3 (p. 7–8), "average of the error metrics per region":

| Variable | RMSE | MAE | MAPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pixel radiance | 0.1561 | 0.7698 | 6.4481 |
| NDVI | 0.0012 | 0.0044 | 1.3014 |
| EVI | 0.0007 | 0.0023 | 1.0069 |
| **PLWQ** | **0.1175** | **0.4878** | **2.7544** |
| Model loss | 0.006814 | | |

Observations the paper draws:
- EVI and NDVI trend downward in NCR, CAR and Region 1 while PLRV trends upward ("increased human activity, population density and growth", p. 6).
- PLWQ "denote[s] an increase in the percentage of the population of that region as poor … Poverty increases with time" (p. 6–7).
- Correlations (Fig. 6, p. 7): PLRV strongly negative with the vegetation indices; the two vegetation indices positively correlated; PLWQ correlated with vegetation indices and **inversely** with PLRV.
- Fig. 7 (p. 8): forecast vs actual PLWQ lines show a "tight fit".

For the reproduction, Lights reports every metric on one stated scale (raw percentage points).

## 7. Stated limitations and future work (p. 8–9)

- Only lights, EVI and NDVI were used; future work could add **land-use/land-cover time series**.
- Multidimensional poverty (education, access to infrastructure; Alkire–Foster) and **asset-based urban features (roads, buildings, houses)** are suggested.
- "**Regional-level data was utilized. Future work could consider conducting a more detailed spatial analysis at the municipal level.**" (p. 9). This sentence is the starting point for Lights.

## 8. What Lights keeps and changes

| Paper | Lights | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Target: DHS PLWQ, regional (17 regions) | **PSA Small Area Estimates of poverty incidence, city/municipal (~1,600 LGUs)**, with their published standard errors | Municipal level is the paper's own future-work item; PSA SAE is open (CC BY), official and comes with uncertainty; DHS needs a data-use agreement and is regional |
| Lights: DMSP-OLS v4, 2004–2013 | **VIIRS** (NOAA annual V2.2 and/or NASA Black Marble), 2012 onward | DMSP ended in 2013 and saturates in cities; VIIRS is radiometrically calibrated and current |
| Vegetation: MOD13Q1 EVI/NDVI | Keep MOD13Q1 as the baseline; add a continuity path (VIIRS VNP13 / Sentinel-2) | Terra is at end of life; see DATA-PLAN.md |
| Monthly series, Prophet-imputed target | **Annual features; the target is never imputed**; the model is trained only on survey years and evaluated on held-out survey years | Lights scores the model only against real survey values |
| 1 GRU layer, Adam 1e-3, batch 32, 100 epochs, random 80/20 | Reproduced exactly as the **baseline**; improved models are compared on **leave-years-out and spatial-block** folds | Keeps the paper as the reference point and adds out-of-sample tests |
| MAE/RMSE/MAPE averaged over regions | Same metrics plus interval coverage, calibration and rank metrics, always on the raw percentage-point scale | One stated scale, plus the uncertainty measures decision-makers need |
